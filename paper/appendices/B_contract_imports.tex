\paragraph{How (I2) is applied.}
In Theorem~\ref{thm:guarded}, second incompleteness is applied only to theories of the form $\EA+\varphi$, with the standard proof predicate of the presentation ``an axiom of $\EA$, or $\varphi$''. It is never applied to an arbitrary formula that merely happens to be called a consistency statement. The applications are to $\EA$ itself in part~(e), to $\EA+\theta$ in~(f), and to $\EA+\varphi$ for consistent $\varphi\in H$ in~(d). In addition, the remark after Theorem~\ref{thm:walsh-class} that $g=\Con_T$ belongs to the restricted class uses second incompleteness for the theories $T+\varphi$, with the corresponding standard consistency sentences.

\paragraph{How (I3) is applied.}
Walsh's theorem is applied to a sound, effectively axiomatized $T\supseteq\EA$, with its hypotheses of recursiveness, monotonicity and bounded range complexity stated explicitly in Theorem~\ref{thm:walsh-class}. The theorem asserts that \emph{some} true cone exists. It is never used to obtain agreement on a cone chosen in advance.

\paragraph{Intended interpretation.}
In the intended reading of Section~\ref{sec:conditional-lift}, the canonical family $\mathcal{K}$ is a family of extensions by consistency or reflection statements~\cite{Turing1939,Feferman1962,BeklemishevReflection,PakhomovWalsh2021,MontalbanWalsh2019}. This reading guides the choice of examples, but no proof depends on it.
